Frequently Asked Questions

How is this different from a basic timezone converter?

A basic timezone converter gives you a number and stops there. This page is built to handle the full job: convert the time, show the real date shift, surface the current local time in each selected region, generate a shareable link, and help you plan a workable meeting window if the conversion turns into a real scheduling decision.

Does this tool handle daylight saving time changes automatically?

Yes. The conversion uses the browser's internationalization data for the date you selected, so the offsets shown are tied to the actual point in time instead of a generic fixed offset.

Can I use the meeting planner for more than two cities?

Yes. The planner supports two-zone and three-zone workflows. If you only need two cities, leave the third input empty. If you need a harder overlap check across three regions, add the third timezone and let the planner surface realistic windows.

What makes UTC different from a local timezone?

UTC is the reference time standard. Local timezones are offsets from UTC and may shift when daylight saving time starts or ends. In practical work, UTC is useful as a neutral anchor, while local time is what people actually use to attend meetings, go on shift, or receive alerts.

Is this timezone converter private?

Yes. The core calculations happen in the browser. Your selected dates, entered values, and timezone choices do not need to leave the page for the main workflow to function.

Why keep live clocks on the same page as the converter?

Because conversion alone is rarely the full problem. People usually want to know what the chosen time means in context. A live clock wall makes it easier to sanity-check whether the converted time lands in normal working hours, late evening, or the next day.
